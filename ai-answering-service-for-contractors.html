The short answer: for most trades, no

The person calling a plumber at seven at night has water coming through a ceiling. They are not going to leave a message and wait. They are going to hang up and call the next number on the list.

The answering service will log that as a captured lead. Your bank account will log it as nothing. Those are two different numbers, and only one of them pays for the truck.

That is the whole case against it, and it gets worse the more urgent your trade is.

What it actually costs you

A captured lead is not a booked job

Every one of these tools reports on messages taken, because that is the number that always goes up. Ask instead how many of those messages became invoices. Nobody puts that on the dashboard.

The emergency calls are the profitable ones

A burst line at 9pm or no heat in January is the job nobody price shops. It is also the call with the least patience for a machine. The tool fails hardest exactly where the money is.

The review follows the call

A homeowner who felt handled by a robot while their basement filled up remembers it. In a trade that runs on word of mouth, that is a cost that shows up months later and never gets traced back.

You pay twice

Once for the subscription, and again for the work it quietly turned away. The subscription is the small number.

When it genuinely does make sense

We would be lying if we said never. There are real cases, and they share a shape: the machine is catching what would otherwise hit an empty line, not standing in front of a person who was going to answer.

  • Overflow, not replacement. Two calls already in progress and a third comes in. Something beats a busy signal.
  • Filtering the junk. If half your ringing is solicitors and marketing agencies, a screen in front of that buys back real hours.
  • Trades where nothing is on fire. Landscaping quotes, remodel enquiries, anything a homeowner is casually shopping. Less urgency, more tolerance.
  • Shops big enough to already have a dispatcher. At that size the calls are triaged anyway and the machine is one more filter, not the front door.

The rule of thumb: the less urgent the call, the better AI does on it. If your phone rings because something broke, you are the worst possible fit.

Common questions

Does TradeDesk have an AI answering service?

No, and it is a deliberate product decision rather than a gap in the roadmap. Our position is that a homeowner calling a contractor wants a human, and that an automated answering system costs more jobs than it captures. If that ever stops being true we will say so here.

What does a missed call actually cost a contractor?

Work out your average job value and how many calls you convert. One missed emergency call in a trade like plumbing or HVAC is usually worth more than a year of any answering subscription, which is exactly why the subscription price is the wrong number to compare against.

Will an AI answering service hurt my reviews?

It can, and the damage is hard to trace because it shows up weeks later in a review that mentions being hard to reach. The risk goes up with urgency: somebody shopping for a deck quote will forgive a robot, somebody whose basement is flooding will not.

What is the difference between an AI answering service and a virtual receptionist?

A virtual receptionist is a person at an answering company. An AI service is software generating replies. The person costs more per call and handles an upset customer far better, so if you are going to hand the phone to somebody else, price both before assuming the cheaper one wins.

Is it worth using one for after hours only?

This is the strongest case for it, because the alternative is a phone nobody picks up. Just be clear about what it is doing: booking genuine emergencies, or taking a message someone still has to action in the morning. Only the first one earns the fee.
