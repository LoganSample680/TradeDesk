# OJT hour tracking, all 50 states plus DC

Research compiled 2026-09-17 for CLAUDE.md §9.7 (Apprentice / Journeyman OJT
Hour Logging), which has always said the state data model is the research piece
and must be done before anything is built. This is that research.

**Verify before relying on any number here.** State licensing rules move, and
several of these moved inside the last twelve months (Texas master plumber 4
years to 2, effective 2025-09-01; Utah S350 to H100 HVAC, 2026-04-20; Maryland
electrical went from county to state licensing in 2021 and HVAC hours changed in
2023; Virginia's forms are dated 07/2026 and 08/2026). Anything shipped from
this must carry the same "check with your state board" disclaimer the tax tool
carries. Entries marked UNCONFIRMED are gaps the research did not close, and are
left as gaps on purpose rather than filled with a plausible guess.

## Method

Five parallel researchers, roughly ten jurisdictions each, working from state
licensing board, department of labor and apprenticeship council sources, pulling
the actual PDF forms wherever they are published. Three trades per state:
electrical, plumbing, HVAC/mechanical, plus a one-line note on any other trade in
that state that requires documented experience before an exam.

Each was asked the same five questions, and told that an honest "could not
confirm" beats a plausible invention:

1. Is the journeyman/master license issued by the state, or left to cities?
2. How many hours (or years) of OJT, for journeyman and for master?
3. Which of three very different things does the state actually want:
   **(a)** a detailed ongoing log the apprentice keeps and files;
   **(b)** an affidavit or verification form attesting to period totals;
   **(c)** nothing beyond a registered apprenticeship program's own records?
4. Who signs it, does their license number go on it, is it notarized?
5. What fields does the form actually carry?

## What the answer turned out to be

**Yes, hours are real.** The common shape is 8,000 hours or four years for
journeyman, and either a term as a licensed journeyman (1 to 4 years) or roughly
double the hours for master. A minority use years and duties with no hour count
at all (Virginia is the clearest: no hours field anywhere on its form).

**No, almost nobody wants a daily log.** Across roughly 120 state-and-trade
combinations, only Iowa's trade-school route requires anything resembling a
running logbook. The distribution:

| What the state wants | Count |
|---|---|
| (b) affidavit / verification of period totals | ~75 |
| (b)+(c) mixed, depending on the route taken | ~18 |
| (a)-leaning hybrid, or periodic filing while training | ~10 |
| (c) registered-apprenticeship program records only | ~4 |
| Nothing, or proved by tax records instead | ~5 |
| Could not confirm | ~7 |

**And no state asks for a job site address or a customer name.** Not one, in any
trade. Every address on every form is the employer's or the applicant's. Boards
want period totals attributable to a named supervisor, not an itinerary.

### The finding that actually matters

The value is not that a board wants your log. It is that **six jurisdictions make
you file while you are still training**, and you cannot produce that filing years
later from memory:

- **Iowa** (PSEP route): monthly. Weekly rows, six work-category columns, signed
  by both the apprentice and a master. Hours not dual-signed "will receive no
  credit."
- **Minnesota**: annually, both electrical and plumbing, one form per employer,
  with a class-of-work split, submitted with the registration renewal.
- **Washington**: plumber trainees annually (notarized, separate affidavit per
  employer, no copies or faxes accepted); electrical trainees every two years,
  within 180 days of certificate expiry, backed by payroll or permit records.
- **Wyoming**: an annual apprentice OJT form, notarized.
- **Colorado**: electrical contractors must maintain per-apprentice work records
  and, since 1 January 2022, file annual apprentice hours and training reports
  with the Board.
- **Oklahoma**: rule 158:40-5-3(g) puts a standing duty on the contractor to
  document each apprentice's hours split by commercial, industrial and
  residential.

Colorado and Oklahoma are the important pair: the legal duty to keep the record
sits on **the contractor**, who is the customer, not on the apprentice.

### Work categories exist, and they never match

Where a category split is required, every state invents its own taxonomy:

- **Texas electrical** (ELC017): a matrix of installed / maintained / extended /
  serviced entrance conductors, crossed with residential / commercial /
  industrial / exempt / other.
- **Texas ACR**: eleven equipment-level checkboxes (new install, replacement, air
  handler, condenser, evaporator, duct work, process pipe, refrigerant and
  process, system sizing, refrigerant drain, other).
- **Texas plumbing** (ECF): six competency attestations, including one for the ADA.
- **Hawaii** (EP-02/EP-03): seven or eight tasks with per-category hour minimums.
- **Iowa**: preliminary / rough-in / finish / troubleshooting and service /
  special systems / motor installation and control, each with minimums.
- **Washington plumbing**: (01) commercial, (02) residential, (03) pump and
  irrigation, (03A) domestic well.
- **South Dakota**: residential-farmstead vs commercial-industrial vs school,
  per license level.
- **Wyoming**: residential, commercial and industrial with a rule that no single
  category may exceed 75% of the total.
- **New Mexico**: underground installation / rough-in / trim out, plus a
  commercial and residential hours split.

A fixed taxonomy will not work. This needs a per-state category map.

### Signatures, license numbers and notaries

- **The supervising licensee's license number is the most consistently required
  field after total hours.** Hawaii, Idaho, Iowa, Maine, Kentucky plumbing,
  Texas, Utah, Washington, New Mexico, Massachusetts and more.
- **Notarization is required in a substantial minority**, including Hawaii (both
  trades), Louisiana plumbing, Washington plumbing, Wyoming electrical, West
  Virginia HVAC, New Mexico, New Jersey and Rhode Island. It is not required in
  Texas, Virginia, Utah, Wisconsin, South Dakota or DC.
- **Who may sign varies far more than the hours do.** Texas wants the supervising
  master's license number, type, effective and expiration dates and issuing
  jurisdiction. Virginia will accept a building inspector or an architect and
  asks for no hours at all. Wisconsin just wants a "witness" with a phone number.
  DC prescribes no form whatsoever, only a letter.
- **Several states reject self-verification outright** (New Mexico, Alabama,
  Texas ACR: "Do NOT complete this form yourself"). Others rest entirely on
  self-certification (Illinois plumbing, Maryland plumbing under penalty of
  perjury).

### Payroll as proof

A distinct group ignores attestation of hours and wants payroll instead:
**Louisiana plumbing** (7,000 or 8,000 hours of official payroll documentation or
four W-2s with matching year-end stubs; 1099s not accepted; no supervisor signs
off on hours at all), **Ohio** (five consecutive years of W-2s or Schedule C plus
a permit pulled each year by the licensed contractor), **California** (a
self-declared category-hour grid corroborated by the applicant's own Social
Security Administration earnings report under AB 1346), and as an accepted
alternative in Kentucky, Idaho, Hawaii and West Virginia.

# The 51 jurisdictions

## ALABAMA
**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (AL Board of Electrical Contractors, AECB). Tiers are Journeyman Electrician and Electrical Contractor; there is no separate "master electrician."
- Journeyman: 8,000 hours documented experience installing electrical wiring/apparatus/equipment. Up to 2,000 hrs may be substituted at 1,000 hrs per year of approved electrical curriculum or apprenticeship (classroom is a substitute, not an additional requirement).
- Master (= Electrical Contractor): 8,000 hours in overall design, planning, layout and direct supervision of electrical construction, or 8,000 hrs as a journeyman in charge of jobs. Same 2,000-hr education substitution. (Rule 303-X-3-.02)
- Log type: **(b) affidavit of totals.** One "Work Affidavit" per employer. No itemized log, no work-category breakdown.
- Signed by: employer or a licensed electrical contractor. Applicant may NOT certify own hours. Signer gives "Lic. # or Title." **Notarized** (notary signature, commission expiry, seal).
- Required fields: affidavit date; applicant last/first/MI; company name; company address/city/state/zip; "in the capacity of"; then per position — Position, from day/month/year, through day/month/year, Total hours worked (two position rows); Total Time Worked; certification that statements are true per company records/personal knowledge; signature; company; Lic.# or Title; phone; fax; email; printed name; notary block. Application also requires 3 non-relative references with knowledge of the applicant's electrical work.
- Form: "Journeyman License by Examination" application, Work Affidavit page (AECB, rev. 9-19-23).
- Source: https://aecb.alabama.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/NEW-JMan-9-19-23.pdf ; https://admincode.legislature.state.al.us/api/rule/303-X-3-.02

**Plumbing** — state-licensed: yes (AL Plumbers and Gas Fitters Examining Board, PGFB)
- Journeyman: minimum 2 years as a registered Apprentice, or a board-approved apprentice training program combined with registered apprentice time equal to 2 years. Board does not publish an hour figure. Classroom: only as part of an approved apprentice program.
- Master: hold a current Journeyman certification a minimum of 1 year.
- Log type: **(c) program/registration records only, for the normal path** — the board registers apprentices annually and counts registered time. For experience gained "through other means," it becomes **(b)**: a Board Appearance Request form with "verifiable documentation of claimed experience," reviewed at a board meeting.
- Signed by: not specified in published material. Could not confirm a named signer, license-number requirement, or notarization.
- Required fields: **could not confirm** — no published itemized experience form located.
- Form: "Board Appearance Request" (alternative-experience path only). No standard hours affidavit found.
- Source: https://pgfb.alabama.gov/applicants/how-do-i-become-licensed/

**HVAC/Mechanical** — state-licensed: yes (AL Board of Heating, Air Conditioning & Refrigeration Contractors, HACR). Contractor-tier only; no state journeyman/master tier.
- Journeyman: n/a (no such license).
- Contractor (Class HVAC and/or Refrigeration): three alternative paths — (1) 2 years (3,000 hrs) under a licensed contractor **plus** board-issued Apprentice Certificates for the previous 2 years; (2) graduation from an approved HVAC/commercial refrigeration curriculum; (3) at least 3,000 hrs (18 months) under a HACR licensee within the past 5 years evidenced by **employer affidavit(s) plus matching W-2 forms**. Also EPA 608, exam, $15,000 bond.
- Log type: **(b) affidavit of totals, corroborated by W-2s.** Apprentice-certificate path is effectively (c).
- Signed by: the employer (HACR licensee). Notarization not confirmed.
- Required fields: **could not confirm** — the affidavit is described as "included in the application packet"; the board's public forms page lists an "EA-4 Employer Verification Form" but the PDF's fields were not retrievable.
- Form: EA-4 Employer Verification Form (listed on board forms page).
- Source: https://hacr.alabama.gov/new-license-application/ ; https://hacr.alabama.gov/forms-and-notices/
**Other licensed trades requiring experience:** Gas Fitter journeyman/master — same PGFB path as plumbing (2 yrs apprentice → journeyman; 1 yr journeyman → master). Refrigeration Contractor — same HACR 3,000-hr rule as HVAC. Other Alabama trades (well drilling, elevator) not researched.

## ALASKA
**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (DOLWD Mechanical Inspection, Certificate of Fitness). Contractor-level "Electrical Administrator" is a separate license under Dept. of Commerce (CBPL).
- Journeyman: 8,000 hours work experience under the NEC. Caps: max 2,000 hrs residential; max 1,000 hrs as power lineman; maintenance-electrician experience does NOT count. Classroom from a USDOL-registered apprenticeship or accredited program may substitute for up to 1,000 of the 8,000 hrs (substitute, not additional). 8 AAC 90.160.
- Master: no "master" COF. Next tier is Electrical Administrator (CBPL) — separate journeyman-experience-plus-management test.
- Log type: **(b) affidavit of totals, but broken out by work category.** Notarized Experience Verification Form (EVF) or notarized employer letters on company letterhead; or a sworn union-official affidavit in lieu.
- Signed by: the **employer** (self-verification explicitly not acceptable). Form captures the representative's "TRADE LICENSE #." **Notarization is mandatory** ("Notarized verification of qualified hours is required for licensing with the State of Alaska"). Alaska-based union officials may sign instead under AS 18.62.050(c) with a union stamp.
- Required fields: applicant last/first/MI + current AK COF#; **electrician hour categories: Commercial/Industrial, Residential (up to 4-plex), Linework (subject to NESC), Classroom Hours (USDOL registered or accredited), Military/Vocational**; company name; address/city/state/zip; printed name of representative; title; trade license #; email; phone; signature; date; notary block (day/month/year, place, commission expiry, notarial or union stamp); certification "I have direct knowledge that the applicant was employed as (job title) ____ under trade license # ____ from ____ to ____ while performing work in the state of ____." Explicit instruction: "DO NOT INCLUDE MAINTENANCE HOURS AND OTHER WORK HOURS"; hours worked in Alaska without a current Trainee COF are rejected. Union affidavit must specify qualifying hours, inclusive dates, type of work performed, and name of each contractor.
- Form: Experience Verification Form (EVF), COF_Application_EVF.pdf, v.20250828 (**recently revised — verify current version**). Application: COF_Application_ELECRICAL-PLUMBING.pdf.
- Source: https://labor.alaska.gov/lss/miforms.htm ; https://labor.alaska.gov/lss/forms/cof/COF_Application_EVF.pdf ; https://labor.alaska.gov/lss/forms/cof-regs.pdf (Information Packet v.20250715)

**Plumbing** — state-licensed: yes (same COF system)
- Journeyman: 8,000 hours legally obtained work experience subject to AS 18.60.705 / 8 AAC 63.010. Caps: max 500 hrs restricted-PU work, max 2,000 hrs restricted-PG work. Up to 1,000 hrs classroom substitution from USDOL-registered apprenticeship or accredited program. 8 AAC 90.130.
- Master: no "master plumber" COF. Next tier is Mechanical Administrator (CBPL): e.g. unlimited commercial/industrial plumbing requires journeyman-plumber practice 4 of the last 6 years **plus** management experience (field superintendent or similar) 4 of the last 6 years; residential HVAC category requires journeyman-level residential HVAC practice 2 of the last 4 years.
- Log type: **(b) affidavit of totals with category breakdown** — same EVF/notarized-letterhead/union-affidavit rule as electrical.
- Signed by: employer, notarized; or Alaska-based union official. Trade license # required on the form.
- Required fields: same EVF as above; **plumber hour categories: Commercial/Residential, Water services / sewer / storm line, Gas piping & appliance, Classroom Hours, Military/Vocational.**
- Form: COF_Application_EVF.pdf (shared electrician/plumber form).
- Source: https://labor.alaska.gov/lss/forms/cof-regs.pdf ; https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/cbpl/professionallicensing/mechanicaladministrators.aspx

**HVAC/Mechanical** — state-licensed: partially. There is **no HVAC certificate of fitness for individual technicians.** The state-level credential is the **Mechanical Administrator** license (Dept. of Commerce, CBPL), required for the contracting firm.
- Journeyman: n/a — no state HVAC journeyman license.
- Mechanical Administrator: by category. Residential HVAC = practical experience as a journeyman in residential HVAC work at least 2 of the 4 years immediately preceding application. Unlimited commercial/industrial plumbing = 4 of 6 years journeyman + 4 of 6 years management. Other categories vary; verify per category in the statutes/regs PDF.
- Log type: **(b)** — "proof satisfactory to the department"; specific form fields not confirmed.
- Signed by: not confirmed.
- Required fields: **could not confirm.**
- Form: Mechanical Administrator License Application (filed through MyAlaska).
- Source: https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/cbpl/ProfessionalLicensing/MechanicalAdministrators/LicenseApplications.aspx ; https://www.commerce.alaska.gov/web/Portals/5/pub/MechStatutes.pdf
**Other licensed trades requiring experience:** Power Lineman Journeyman COF (same 8,000-hr EVF structure, NESC linework category); Residential Wireman COF; Plumber Gas and Plumber Utility COFs; Asbestos Abatement; Hazardous Painter; Explosive Handler; Boiler Operator Class 1-4; Amusement/Tramway/Boiler Inspector — all COF-based through the same Mechanical Inspection Section. Electrical Administrator (CBPL) parallel to Mechanical Administrator.

## ARIZONA
**Electrical** — state-licensed: **no journeyman/master license at state level.** Arizona licenses only the contracting business and its Qualifying Party through the Registrar of Contractors (ROC). A.R.S. § 32-1122 contains no journeyman or master trade-license prerequisite. Municipal journeyman cards, where they exist, are local; not researched.
- Journeyman: n/a.
- Qualifying Party (the state-level credential): minimum **4 years practical or management trade experience** in the type of construction applied for, **at least 2 of which must be within the last 10 years**. Up to 2 years of accredited technical training may substitute but cannot satisfy the whole requirement. Two exams (trade + business management). The registrar waives experience documentation if the person is or has been a qualifying party in the same classification.
- Log type: **(b), and weakly so — self-declared experience statement plus a named reference.** No employer signature on the experience section and no itemized log.
- Signed by: nobody countersigns the experience block. A "reference" with direct first-hand knowledge is named with contact details (cannot be a person named on the application); ROC contacts them. The application's bond/attorney-in-fact pages are notarized, **the experience section is not.**
- Required fields (per Relevant Experience block): 1. Business Name of Employer or "Self-Employed"; 2. Duration of Experience (date range); 3. **Average Hours Worked Per Week**; 4. Position(s); 5. **Size of Projects Worked On (square foot and/or dollar amount)**; 6. Description of Qualifying Party's Main Duties. Experience Reference block: reference's name; relationship to qualifying party; mailing address/city/state/zip; phone; email. Additional pages as needed. ROC may also ask for W-2s/tax returns and verification letters.
- Form: RC-L-201D Application for a New Qualifying Party (and RC-L-200A license application, same experience block).
- Source: https://www.azleg.gov/ars/32/01122.htm ; https://roc.az.gov/sites/default/files/2024-02/RC-L201D%20Application%20for%20a%20New%20Qualifying%20Party.pdf (direct fetch blocked 403; block text confirmed from the ROC RC-L-200A application)
**Plumbing** — identical: no state journeyman/master license; ROC qualifying-party 4-year rule and the same experience block.
**HVAC/Mechanical** — identical: no state journeyman/master license; ROC qualifying-party 4-year rule and the same experience block.
**Other licensed trades requiring experience:** every ROC classification carries its own experience requirement set by the License Classification Requirements table, all documented through the same block. No separate state journeyman-type license found for any trade. (Not exhaustively checked outside ROC.)

## ARKANSAS
**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (AR Board of Electrical Examiners, Dept. of Labor and Licensing)
- Journeyman: complete a **USDOL-certified apprenticeship with 8,000 hours OJT**, or 16,000 hours qualifying OJT in an out-of-state licensed jurisdiction (unless that state reciprocates). Residential Journeyman: 2 years USDOL apprenticeship + 4,000 hrs (does not reciprocate). Apprenticeship programs carry roughly 800 hrs of technical instruction; hours are capped at **2,000/year maximum, overtime not allowed.**
- Master: **12,000 hours** qualifying OJT via completed USDOL apprenticeship, or 16,000 hours out-of-state. Residential Master: 2 years USDOL apprenticeship + 8,000 hrs (does not reciprocate).
- Log type: **(b) affidavit of totals, with a mandatory work-category breakdown.** "Verification of hours must be on this form to be acknowledged; any other forms of verification will not be accepted." A separate form per employer.
- Signed by: the **Contracting Master Electrician**, signed in the presence of a notary; the form carries "Master License Holder Name" and "Master License Number." **Notarization required** (state/county, notary stamp, commission expiry).
- Required fields: applicant name (first/last/MI); SSN; address/city/state/zip; Name of Electrical Contractor; email; contractor address/city/state/zip; Master License Holder Name; Master License Number; phone; **Position Held by Applicant**; Dates of Employment (start/end); **Total Hours of Electrical Work**; **"Dates Above Taken from Payroll Records — Yes/No, explain"**; **Type of Electrical Work Performed with hours per category: Residential Wiring ___ / Commercial Construction ___ / Industrial Construction ___ / Industrial Maintenance ___ / Sign Specialist ___**; declaration; master's signature + date; notary block. Military: attach DD-214 plus service record of job duties and training certifications.
- Form: **ELECTRICIAN EMPLOYMENT VERIFICATION, ABOEE (1-2025)** — attached to the online Application for Electrician's Exam Clearance.
- Source: https://labor.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/Employment_Verification-AR-2.0.pdf ; https://labor.arkansas.gov/labor/code-enforcement/electrical-inspection-and-licensing/

**Plumbing** — state-licensed: yes (AR Dept. of Health, State Committee of Plumbing Examiners)
- Journeyman: minimum **4 years' experience or its equivalent**. Apprentice path: register with ADH, complete the Arkansas plumber apprenticeship (8,000 hrs OJT over ~4 years) plus not less than 160 hours/year of related classroom instruction approved by the State Apprenticeship Office/USDOL.
- Master: minimum **5 years' experience**, and must hold the Journeyman license **1 full year** before becoming eligible for the Master exam (hardship waiver possible).
- Log type: **(c) for the apprenticeship path** (successful completion of the apprenticeship under Ark. Code Ann. § 17-38-409 is what the Committee considers). For applicants with no licensing background it is **(b), and deliberately loose**: the Committee "shall use the least restrictive requirements by allowing the licensing candidate to show proof of experience in the form of **records, affidavits, or bona fide evidence** from current or former employers, or persons who can attest to the applicant's work background as a plumber."
- Signed by: current/former employers or persons with knowledge. No named signer requirement, no license-number requirement, no notarization requirement in the rule.
- Required fields: **none prescribed by rule** — the rule deliberately does not specify a form or fields. Affidavit only, of the applicant's employment history.
- Form: no prescribed experience form found in the licensing rules.
- Source: https://healthy.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/Plumbing-Licensing-Rules.pdf (Section III(a), Master/Journeyman/Restricted Plumber Qualifications)

**HVAC/Mechanical** — state-licensed: yes (AR HVACR Board, Dept. of Labor and Licensing). Contractor-class only; Arkansas explicitly does **not** use the apprentice→journeyman→master ladder.
- Journeyman: n/a. Classes: A (unlimited), B (≤15 tons / 1M BTUH per unit), C (service/repair only), D (sheet metal ductwork), E (refrigeration).
- Licensure: applicants with no licensing background must present "at least **two (2) years** experience as an HVACR contractor or an employee of an HVACR contractor." The Board may substitute approved educational training (transcripts required). Statute bars apprenticeship/education/training from being a **prerequisite** to licensure. Note: secondary sources commonly cite 4 years/8,000 hrs for Class A and 2 years/4,000 for Class B — **the board's own 2023 application says 2 years for all classes; treat the 4-year figure as unverified.** Non-licensee employees must be registered.
- Log type: **(b) affidavit of totals with work-category breakdown.** "A separate affidavit must be completed for each employer work experience is claimed."
- Signed by: the **employer**, under oath; form carries employer License Type and License #. **Notarized** ("Subscribed and sworn to before me…").
- Required fields: applicant name (first/last/middle); applicant address/city/state/zip; email; phone; dates of employment verification (MM/YYYY from–to). Employer block: first/last/MI; License Type; License #; email; Company Name; phone; address and mailing address/city/state/zip; dates of employment (from–to); **Applicant Job Duties (Be Specific)**; **Number of hours in each work area — Residential ___ / Commercial ___ / Refrigeration ___ / Ductwork ___ / Total Hours Worked ___**; sworn statement; employer signature + printed name; company name; title; notary block.
- Form: HVACR License Application, employer affidavit page (Rev. 8/23).
- Source: https://www.labor.arkansas.gov/wp-content/uploads/HVAC-Contractor-Application-2023.pdf
**Other licensed trades requiring experience:** HVACR Class D (sheet metal/ductwork) and Class E (refrigeration) — same 2-year rule and same category affidavit. Plumbing board also licenses Restricted Gas Fitter, Restricted Hospital Maintenance, Water & Sewer Service Line Installation, Restricted Solar Mechanic, and Plumbing & Gas Inspector — all under the same "records, affidavits, or bona fide evidence" standard. Electrical board also issues Residential Master and Residential Journeyman (4,000/8,000 hrs) and a Sign Specialist hour category.

## CALIFORNIA
**Electrical** — state-licensed: **yes, two separate credentials.** (1) Individual **Electrician Certification** from DIR/DLSE for anyone doing electrical work for a C-10 contractor. (2) **C-10 Electrical contractor license** from CSLB. There is no "master electrician."
- Journeyman (DIR certification, by category): **General Electrician 8,000 hrs; Residential Electrician 4,800; Fire/Life Safety Technician 4,000; Voice Data Video Technician 4,000; Nonresidential Lighting Technician 2,000.** Holders of a state-approved apprenticeship certificate bypass the hour tally.
- Master (CSLB C-10 contractor): **4 years journey-level or higher experience** within the last 10 years, in the classification applied for. Experience must be at journeyman, foreman, supervising employee, or contractor level.
- Log type: **hybrid and unusual.** DIR: **(b)-style self-declared category-hour grid under penalty of perjury, corroborated not by an employer affidavit but by the applicant's own Social Security Administration earnings report (SSA-7050)** — first-time applicants must submit it (AB 1346). Foreign experience uses employer verification letters. CSLB: **(b) affidavit of totals plus a narrative trade-duty list**, verified by a third-party certifier.
- Signed by: DIR — the **applicant only**, "under penalty of perjury"; no employer signature, no notary. CSLB — a **certifier** who is an employer, fellow employee, journeyman, union representative, contractor, or business associate with "direct knowledge"; original wet signature required (no fax/photocopy/stamp); contractor certifiers give their license number; **under penalty of perjury, not notarized.** A licensee who signs a falsified form faces discipline (B&PC 7114.1). 3% of applications are randomly audited against payroll records.
- Required fields — DIR form DLSE-ECF1: name, mailing address, phone, email, DOB, driver's license state/#; proof-of-experience path checkbox; **category-hour grid with per-category maximums** — Stock Room and Material Handling (max 300), Residential Wiring (max 3,000), Commercial Wiring (max 6,000), Industrial Wiring (max 6,000), Voice Data and Video Installation (max 1,500), Underground Conduit Installation (max 750), Troubleshooting and Maintenance (max 1,500), Finish Work and Fixtures (max 600), Fire/Life Safety & Nurse Call (max 600), plus separate columns/caps per exam type; Total Credit Hours; exam type checkboxes; language; signature + date.
- Required fields — CSLB 13A-11: Part 1 (applicant) — qualifier's full legal name; business name of company where experience was gained (or self-employed checkbox); **license number of company**; company street address (no P.O. boxes); owner-builder yes/no. Part 2 (certifier) — full-time/part-time checkbox; from-date, to-date, total years and months; **"list ALL specific trade duties applicant performed or supervised in the classification"**; certifier's business relationship (employer / contractor + license number / foreman or supervisor / journeyman / fellow employee / union representative / business associate); certifier street address, city, state, ZIP, phone, fax, email; penalty-of-perjury certification, date, signature, printed name.
- Form: **DLSE-ECF1** (Application for Electrician Examination and Certification) and **CSLB 13A-11** (Certification of Work Experience, rev. 12/2024).
- Source: https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/ecu/ElectricalCertificationForm.pdf ; https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/ecu/ElectricianApplicationInstructions.html ; https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/FormsAndApplications/CertificationOfWorkExperience.pdf

**Plumbing** — state-licensed: **contractor only.** California has **no journeyman or master plumber license.** Only the CSLB **C-36 Plumbing** contractor license.
- Journeyman: n/a.
- Master (C-36): 4 years journey-level or higher experience within the last 10 years.
- Log type: **(b) affidavit of totals + trade-duty narrative** (CSLB 13A-11).
- Signed by: certifier with direct knowledge (employer, fellow employee, journeyman, union rep, contractor, business associate); contractor certifiers give license #; penalty of perjury; **no notary.**
- Required fields: identical to CSLB 13A-11 above.
- Form: CSLB 13A-11.
- Source: https://www.cslb.ca.gov/Resources/FormsAndApplications/CertificationOfWorkExperience.pdf

**HVAC/Mechanical** — state-licensed: **contractor only.** No journeyman or master HVAC license. CSLB **C-20 Warm-Air Heating, Ventilating and Air-Conditioning**.
- Journeyman: n/a.
- Master (C-20): 4 years journey-level or higher experience within the last 10 years.
- Log type: **(b)** — CSLB 13A-11.
- Signed by: as above.
- Required fields: identical to CSLB 13A-11.

## COLORADO
**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (DORA, State Electrical Board). Credentials: Residential Wireman, Journeyman, Master, Electrical Contractor.

## DELAWARE
**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (Board of Electrical Examiners, Division of Professional Regulation). Tiers: Apprentice, Journeyperson, Master, Limited, Master Special, Limited Special.
- Journeyperson: completion of a Board-approved apprenticeship program including its final exam, **or** over **8,000 hours** of full-time experience performing electrical work under the supervision of a licensed master electrician, master electrician special, limited electrician, or limited electrician special. Delaware apprenticeships run 4 years OJT with **576 hours** related instruction.
- Master: **6 years' full-time experience** under a licensed master electrician; **or 8,000 hours** full-time under a licensed master **plus 576 hours related instruction** verified by a certificate of completion of apprenticeship from a bona fide registered apprenticeship program of any state; **or 4 years' full-time experience** under a licensed master **plus 2 years' technical training**. - Log type: **(b) affidavit of totals, with a work-description field.** Statute: "All evidence of experience shall be submitted on written affidavit forms provided by the Board."

Do not email or fax.
- Required fields: Application ID; applicant name; employer company name, street, city, state, ZIP. Supervisor section — supervisor's full name; all jurisdictions where the applicant obtained experience; supervisor's license type (e.g. Master), license number and jurisdiction at the time of supervision. Supervised experience — checkbox per level: **Apprentice** from (m/d/y) to (m/d/y) **Total Hours Worked**, applicant's apprentice license number + jurisdiction; **Journeyperson** from/to, **Total Hours Worked**, applicant's journeyperson license number + jurisdiction; separate line for the period the applicant worked **full-time (35+ hours per week)** with total hours; **"Describe the types of electrical work the applicant performed under your supervision."** Affidavit under penalty of perjury; supervisor signature + date; county/state; notary signature, seal, commission expiry.
- Form: **Electrical_Verification_of_Employment.pdf** (rev. 10/2021).

## FLORIDA
**Electrical** — state-licensed: **contractor only.** Florida issues **no statewide journeyman electrician license.** The Electrical Contractors Licensing Board (ECLB) issues Certified (statewide) and Registered (local-jurisdiction) electrical contractor licenses. Journeyman cards, where they exist, are issued by counties and municipalities — local-only, not researched.

- Master (Certified Electrical Contractor, § 489.511(2)): one of — (a) within the **6 years** preceding application, at least **3 years proven management experience** in the trade or educational equivalent, not more than half educational; (b) within **8 years**, at least **4 years as a supervisor or contractor** in the trade (or equivalent military supervisory experience); (c) within **12 years**, at least **6 years of comprehensive training, technical education, or supervisory experience** with an electrical or alarm contracting business; (d) 3 years as a licensed PE qualified in electrical engineering within 12 years; (e) any combination totaling 6 years. ## GEORGIA
**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (State Construction Industry Licensing Board, Division of Electrical Contractors, under the Secretary of State). Statewide Class I (restricted, single-phase ≤200 A at the service) and Class II (unrestricted). **No journeyman electrician license** — Georgia licenses electrical contractors only.
- Journeyman: n/a — no such license.
- Master (Electrical Contractor Class I or II): minimum **4 years of Primary Experience** in the electrical field as covered by the Georgia/National Electrical Code; at least 21 years old; exam score ≥70. Up to **1 year** may be credited from Secondary Experience (credited at 50%) or education (a 2-year academic diploma or vocational-technical certificate program counts as 2 years, capped by the 1-year rule). Class II applicants must additionally document experience above single-phase 200 A. Applicants who can demonstrate **6 years of electrical contracting experience** need not demonstrate Primary Experience gained by direct installation.
- Log type: **(b) affidavit/narrative of experience plus notarized third-party references — with a mandated work-category checklist.** No ongoing log.
- Signed by: the application itself is **notarized**. In addition, **three references on the required form** from persons who can attest to the applicant's electrical experience; **at least one must be from a licensed electrical contractor, who must include his or her registration number on the form.** Reference forms must be notarized originals; copies are not accepted.
- Required fields: employer information beginning with the current employer — **qualifying licensee name, license holder's license type, dates of employment, and a brief description of duties**; plus documented experience in the statutory **work areas**: Class I applicants must document at least **five of (a)–(g) plus (h)**; Class II must document **all** of: (a) raceway systems (pull boxes, junction boxes, conduit bodies, connections to cabinets/panelboards/switchboards/boxes); (b) conductors (flexible cords, cables, splices, taps, terminations, bonding jumpers, overcurrent devices, metering devices; sizing, ampacity, voltage); (c) service entrances, metering devices, cabinets, switchboards, service risers and fasteners, overcurrent devices, disconnecting means, ground electrodes, main bonding jumpers, ground fault protectors; (d) motors and generators with feeders, branch circuits, overcurrent devices, disconnects, controllers; (e) switches, disconnects and controls for lighting, appliances, circuits, HVAC controls, general-use equipment; (f) special-occupancy materials and equipment per NEC Chapter 5; (g) bonding of interior metal piping, equipment grounding conductors, grounding of exposed non-current-carrying metal parts, physical protection of grounding conductors; (h) load calculations — general lighting loads, minimum branch circuits, minimum ampacity of feeder and branch conductors, maximum raceway conductor fill, net loads by specified or optional methods, demand and derating factors. Plus a nationwide criminal background check.
- Form: Application for Statewide Electrical Contractor License + 3 notarized Personal Reference Forms. (sos.ga.gov form PDFs return 403 to automated fetch; requirements confirmed from the rule text and the board's own application instructions.)

**Plumbing** — state-licensed: yes (same Board, Division of Master Plumbers and Journeyman Plumbers). All persons performing plumbing work, including employees, must hold a Master or Journeyman license.
- Journeyman: minimum **1 year of experience** in plumbing work covered by the Georgia State Plumbing Code. That year may be Primary **or** Secondary experience. (This is by far the lowest journeyman bar of the ten states.)
- Master: minimum **5 years of experience**, **with at least 2 of the 5 years licensed as a Journeyman Plumber**. - Log type: **(b) affidavit/narrative of experience plus licensed-plumber references.** No ongoing log, no hour count anywhere in the rule.
- Signed by: the applicant must **provide the names of three persons licensed as plumbers** who can attest to the experience. **Class I: at least 1 of the 3 must be a licensed Master Plumber. Class II: at least 2 must be licensed Master Plumbers.** Application is notarized; reference forms are notarized originals.
- Required fields: employment history with employer/qualifying licensee name, license type, dates of employment and description of duties; classification of experience as Primary (direct installation of plumbing systems under supervision of a licensed Master Plumber — including work as plumbing contractor, master, journeyman, plumbing foreman, plumbing superintendent, or a military plumber of rank ≥ E-4/3rd class petty officer) or Secondary (plumbing work as a maintenance employee of a county water or public works department; plumbing inspection by a full-time municipal or county inspector); three licensed-plumber references with license numbers.
- Form: Application for Statewide Master / Journeyman Plumber License + notarized reference forms.
- Source: https://rules.sos.ga.gov/gac/121-3-.02 (Rule 121-3-.02, renumbered from 121-2-.02 effective 19 Mar 2025)

**HVAC/Mechanical** — state-licensed: yes (same Board, Division of Conditioned Air Contractors). Class I restricted (≤175,000 BTU heating / 60,000 BTU cooling) and Class II unrestricted. **No journeyman tier.**

Copies are not accepted.

- Source: https://rules.sos.ga.gov/gac/121-3-.04 (formerly 121-2-.03) ; https://sos.ga.gov/sites/default/files/forms/45%20Application%20-%20Conditioned%20Air%20EXAM_0.pdf (403 to automated fetch; content confirmed from the board's published application packet)

## MINNESOTA
*(Minnesota is the most structured of the ten: experience is credited **by class of work**, verified **annually** by the employer, and must be backed by payroll records. This is the closest thing in your ten states to a real ongoing log.)*

**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (Dept. of Labor and Industry, Construction Codes and Licensing Division)

Employer name; license/registration number; employer address; telephone; city/state/zip; email. Name of responsible individual; license number; title. Exact dates of employment (month/day/year) start and end. "Are the hours reported on this form taken from payroll records? YES / OTHER (specify)". Then **hours worked per CLASS OF WORK**: planning; laying out; supervising; wiring for and installing; maintaining and repairing; line work; installing elevators; technology circuits/systems; process control circuits/systems. Total of all qualifying hours worked (max 2,000/yr). Responsible person's signature + date; applicant's signature + date. **No job-site address, no customer name.** Separate form per employer and per year
- Form: "Electrical Work Experience Verification Form" (registered-unlicensed version: https://dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/RegUnlicensedExp.pdf); parallel forms exist for Power Limited Technician (https://dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/RT_exp.pdf) and Elevator (https://www.dli.mn.gov/sites/default/files/pdf/elev_experience.pdf)

## NEBRASKA

## NEVADA

### Cross-cutting notes for the OJT-hours feature design

1. **Three genuinely different data models showed up, and they are not interchangeable.** (i) Minnesota is a real **class-of-work hours ledger**, filed annually and backed by payroll records — MN electrical has 9 classes, MN plumbing 5 (WD/DW/FI/MR/WS), MN pipefitting/elevator/power-limited each have their own. (ii) Michigan, Montana, Nevada, Massachusetts are **per-employer affidavits of totals**, sometimes with a 2-bucket split (Montana: residential vs commercial/industrial/institutional; Massachusetts: % practical vs % other). (iii) New Hampshire plumbing and Montana's apprenticeship route are **program records only** — a completion certificate, nothing else.
2. **Nobody asks for a job-site address or a customer name.** Not one of the ten states' forms I actually opened has either field. If you build address/customer capture off the geo-fence, that is a product feature, not a compliance requirement — the compliance payload is dates, hours, class of work, employer, supervisor license number.
3. **Payroll records are the universal backstop.** MA ("can payroll records be produced for this employee?"), MN ("are the hours reported taken from payroll records? YES/OTHER"), MI (notarized employer statement), KC Missouri (W-2s, pay stubs, 1099s). A time log that can export alongside payroll is worth more than one that cannot.
4. **Notarization splits the field:** required in Michigan (employer signature) and New Jersey (applicant affidavit); explicitly **not** required in Nevada, Minnesota, Montana (in-state), or Mississippi (MSBOC dropped it). Build it as a per-jurisdiction flag.
5. **Signer identity varies more than hours do.** MA needs *two* signatures (master of record + supervising journeyman). MN needs the employer's **designated responsible individual whose license number matches the state's record**, plus the applicant. Montana explicitly bars union records and bars self-verification. Nevada accepts any certifier with firsthand knowledge and doesn't require their license number. Nebraska requires **no** signer at all.
6. **Recently changed / verify before shipping:** New Jersey's June 2, 2025 conversion of "qualified journeyman registration" to "Class A journeyman license" (forms still say the old name); Michigan's plumbing apprentice ratios changed 4/2/2025; Michigan's BCC-3923 is rev. 1/2025.
7. **Explicit gaps I did not close** (do not let anyone fill these from a neighbouring state): MA plumbing Statement of Experience field list; MA refrigeration technician form; Michigan mechanical contractor documentation; every Mississippi and Missouri municipal form; Montana master electrician hours; Nebraska and Minnesota municipal plumbing/HVAC; NH gas fitter requirements and both NH form field lists; NJ master plumber and NJ HVACR form field lists. mass.gov, michigan.gov (HTML), oplc.nh.gov, kcmo.gov and stlouiscountymo.gov all block automated retrieval — those need a human with a browser.## NEW MEXICO

## OREGON
**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (Building Codes Division, BCD; apprenticeships registered via BOLI)

Also SIG (sign), ST (stage), BME (building maintenance), LRT (renewable energy, Oregon apprenticeship only).

### Cross-cutting notes worth carrying into the product design

1. **Nobody requires a daily log.** Across all ten states, not one licensing authority requires an ongoing daily or weekly apprentice logbook submitted to the board. Every state is either (b) an affidavit of totals or (c) registered-apprenticeship program records. The closest thing to (a) is **Oklahoma rule 158:40-5-3(g)**, which puts a standing duty on the *contractor* to document each apprentice's hours split by commercial, industrial and residential. That is the single best hook for a "your records already satisfy this" pitch.
2. **The category split is the real data requirement, and it varies by state.** Oregon plumbing wants 8 named categories x residential/commercial hours. Oregon electrical wants ~9 named categories with a duty narrative each. Oklahoma wants commercial/industrial/residential/other as percentages. New Mexico wants commercial vs residential hours plus rough-in/trim-out style checkboxes. North Carolina plumbing/HVAC wants a single % installation vs % maintenance-service-repair split. A capture model that tags every time entry with (a) a trade category from a per-state list and (b) a residential/commercial/industrial flag would satisfy all of them.
3. **Job-site address is almost never required.** Only NYC asks for premises/permit information, and SC Doc. 167 asks for the customer contact. Everywhere else the unit of record is *employer + date range + hours*, not *job*. Do not over-build address capture for this.
4. **Supervisor licence number is required in 7 of 10**: NM, NC (plumbing 1-B), ND, OK, NYC, Allegheny County PA, and RI's ratio sheet. Oregon asks for "License numbers" but does not gate on it.
5. **Notarization is required in NM, NC, ND, OK, NYC and RI. It is NOT required in Oregon.** Ohio requires no experience attestation at all.

- Form: #3106 Journeyman Electrician (+ #3105 Industrial, #3111 Residential, #3107 Master, #3112 Residential Master) — https://dsps.wi.gov/Credentialing/Trades/fm3106.pdf
- Source: https://docs.legis.wisconsin.gov/code/admin_code/sps/safety_and_buildings_and_environment/301_319/305/IV/443

**Plumbing** — state-licensed: yes (DSPS)
- Journeyman: complete a recognized plumbing apprenticeship (5-year program, minimum 576 hrs plumbing-related instruction) under Wis Stat 106.025 or the federal DOL; certificate of completion or equivalency from the Wisconsin Bureau of Apprenticeship Standards.
- Master: at least 1,000 hrs/yr as a licensed Wisconsin Journeyman Plumber for at least 3 yrs.
- Log type: (c) + (b). Apprenticeship certificate plus an experience table of totals.
- Signed by: a WITNESS. "To be completed by applicant, journeyman plumber, or witness that holds equivalent issued by another state." NO notary.
- Required fields (exact, six rows): Began; End; Hours; Name of Witness; Signature. Plus "Is there any disciplinary action pending against the applicant?"
- Form: #3126 Journeyman Plumber, #3129 Master Plumber — https://dsps.wi.gov/Credentialing/Trades/fm3126.pdf

**HVAC/Mechanical** — optional state certification, no mandatory individual license.
- HVAC Qualifier Certification (DSPS): 4 yrs of approved HVAC-related education or documented work experience at at least 1,000 hrs/yr, or a combination; plus an open-book state exam (70%). An HVAC Contractor Registration is required for the business, which must employ a certified HVAC qualifier. Municipalities may impose their own license if you lack the state certificate.
- Log type: (b) documented experience on the application. Field list not extracted; unconfirmed.
- Form: #3122 HVAC Qualifier Certification Application — https://dsps.wi.gov/Credentialing/Trades/fm3122.pdf

**Other:** DSPS Trades Credentialing also covers elevator mechanic, automatic fire sprinkler fitter, well driller and pump installer, soil tester. Not verified.

## WYOMING
**Electrical** — state-licensed: yes (Wyoming State Fire Marshal, Electrical Safety). The ONLY one of the three trades Wyoming licenses statewide.
- Apprentice: registered, reports OJT on a YEARLY schedule. Year 1: 0-1,999 OJT hrs + 0-143 school hrs, escalating to Year 5: 8,000-10,000 OJT + 576+ school hrs.
- Journeyman: 4 yrs / 8,000 hrs + 576 classroom hrs. COMPOSITION RULE: must include residential, commercial AND industrial work, with NO MORE THAN 75% IN ANY ONE CATEGORY. A genuine work-category requirement, not just a total.
- Master: 8 yrs / 16,000 hrs, of which 4 yrs must be documented as a licensed journeyman, + 576 classroom hrs.
- Low voltage/limited: LV generally 2 yrs / 4,000 hrs; LV-A (alarm) 3,000 hrs; LV-LS (lawn sprinkler) 80 hrs.
- Log type: (a) FOR APPRENTICES (an annual OJT form filed while training); (b) for journeyman and master (a work verification form, or notarized employer letters).
- Signed by: the reporting contractor, licensed journeyman, or master electrician, and THE SIGNATURE MUST BE NOTARIZED. Alternatives: notarized letters from past and present employers, notarized letters from local unions, or official letters from other licensing boards.
- Required fields: NOT EXTRACTED; unconfirmed. Given the 75% composition rule, the journeyman/master form almost certainly breaks hours into residential/commercial/industrial, but this was not verified against the form.
- Forms: Apprentice Electrician OJT Form; Apprentice Low Voltage/Limited OJT Form; Journeyman and Master Work Verification Form
- Source: https://wsfm.wyo.gov/electrical-safety/licensing

**Plumbing** — state-licensed: NO. Local only.
- Casper: Journeyman = certificate of completion of a federally recognized apprenticeship, OR 4 yrs working at a City of Casper licensed shop in that trade; plus proof of ICC testing (G27-N, G28-N, G29-N or G31-N by specialty). Master = federally recognized apprenticeship OR 4 yrs at a Casper licensed shop, PLUS 5 yrs as journeyman/master at such a shop, plus testing.
- Cheyenne: Contractor Licensing Board issues apprentice, journeyman and qualified supervisor/master plumber; journeyman commonly requires apprenticeship completion plus roughly 8,000 hrs and a local exam.
- Jackson, Gillette, Cody, Kemmerer: individual trade licenses required, with ICC certification.
- Source: https://www.casperwy.gov/services/building_and_inspections/contractor_licensing/individual_contractors.php

**HVAC/Mechanical** — state-licensed: NO. Local only. Casper issues Mechanical/HVAC Contractor, Master, Journeyman and Apprentice on the same structure. Cheyenne, Jackson Hole, Kemmerer, Cody, Gillette also require individual HVAC trade licenses with ICC certification.

**Other:** Casper also licenses Gas Utility, Utility/Sewer & Water (installer requires minimum 1 yr in the utility trade), Sanitary Sewer Cleaning, Water Conditioning. Statewide, the Fire Marshal licenses low-voltage/limited technicians (LV, LV-A, LV-LS).

## DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA
**Electrical** — licensed by DC (DLCP, Board of Industrial Trades). Apprentice, Journeyman, Master, Contractor.
- Journeyman: work as an apprentice electrician at least 8,000 hrs over at least 4 yrs; OR a degree in electrical engineering plus at least 2 yrs practical experience CERTIFIED BY A LICENSED MASTER ELECTRICIAN. Exam waived on proof of completion of a nationally certified apprenticeship program. NEC Code Class Certification required within 2 yrs of licensure.
- Master: 7 yrs practical experience as a journeyman (5 yrs with an electrical engineering degree) under a DC master electrician. Sourced from a DCMR summary; dcrules.elaws.us returned 503 and 17-216/17-218 could not be read directly. TREAT MASTER FIGURES AS UNCONFIRMED.
- Log type: (b), THE LOOSEST IN THIS ENTIRE SET: a free-form employment verification LETTER, not a state form.
- Signed by: the employer / a licensed master. DLCP instruction: "Submit employment verification letter(s) signed ... Letters should state the length of time employed and experience." No prescribed form, no field list, no notary mentioned.
- Source: https://dlcp.dc.gov/page/board-industrial-trades1 ; https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-2853.92

**Plumbing (and Gasfitting)** — licensed by DC (DC Code 47-2853.122)
- Journeyman plumber or gasfitter: apprentice at least 8,000 hrs over at least 4 yrs; OR mechanical engineering degree plus at least 2 yrs practical experience AS VERIFIED BY A LICENSED MASTER PLUMBER OR MASTER GASFITTER; OR a certificate from a NATIONAL CERTIFYING ORGANIZATION designating the applicant a journeyman.
- Master: valid journeyman license AND worked as a journeyman at least 4 yrs.
- Log type: (b) employment verification letter, or (c) national apprenticeship certificate of completion.
- Required fields: none prescribed; letter must state length of time employed and experience.
- Source: https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-2853.122

**HVAC/Mechanical (Refrigeration & Air Conditioning Mechanic)** — licensed by DC (DC Code 47-2853.202)
- Journeyman RAC mechanic: employed installing, maintaining, repairing and replacing refrigeration and AC systems LESS THAN 25 COMPRESSOR HORSEPOWER for AT LEAST 5 CONSECUTIVE YEARS IMMEDIATELY PRECEDING the date of application, as verified in writing by a master mechanic; plus CFC/EPA 608.
- Master RAC mechanic: same, but systems LARGER than 25 compressor horsepower, 5 consecutive years, verified in writing by a master mechanic.
- NOTE: a RECENCY AND EQUIPMENT-SIZE rule, not an hour count. "Consecutive years immediately preceding" is unusual and worth designing for.
- Log type: (b) written verification by a master mechanic. Exam can be waived on completion of an apprenticeship program.
- Source: https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/47-2853.202

**Other:** Board of Industrial Trades also covers Asbestos Workers, Steam and Operating Engineers, and Elevator trades. Same employment-verification-letter model.
